## Current positioning

Ben is now independent, based in Charleston, South Carolina, and seeking his next role. His earlier management work at EY is career evidence, not current employment. He explicitly made his blog and its content at [bendewey.wordpress.com](https://bendewey.wordpress.com/) available for this site work. Treat that as authorization to assess and reuse his own published material, with original dates and attribution. Do not imply current publishing frequency or an active speaking calendar.

The narrative should explain a progression from hands-on technical practice and public knowledge sharing into product and large-team leadership, alongside the founder work already established in the brief. The public-facing phrasing should be confident rather than apologetic. Suggested About copy:

> I’m an independent product and technology leader in Charleston, South Carolina, seeking my next opportunity. My background spans software development, user experience, product leadership, and sharing technical knowledge through writing and speaking.

Do not infer team counts, reporting structures, dates, budgets, or outcomes from “large teams.” Those details still need specific evidence for case studies.

**Homepage priority:** personal introduction and current availability → selected work and role evidence → approach to leading teams and products → selected earlier contributions → Nuology founder connection → résumé/contact. Use “Selected writing & talks” or “Writing & resources,” not a prominent reverse-chronological latest-post feed. Preserve original publication dates; introduce older material once as the technical and community foundation of the career. No new publishing commitment is needed for launch.

The three visual directions remain viable, but their framing changes: the Practitioner’s Portfolio emphasizes all-sans typography and leadership; the Workshop emphasizes how Ben leads product and delivery teams, with technical artifacts as supporting evidence; the Forum becomes a career retrospective with public contributions rather than a current speaker-booking brand. The original interactive previews are superseded: they use serif typography and must not be used as a visual baseline.

### Synthesis

The common content is a recognizable person, a clear area of contribution, proof through published material, and simple routes to contact or deeper work. The designs are varied; there is no single “Microsoft MVP look.” Technical credibility does not require a blue palette, terminal aesthetic, animated code, or a Microsoft-style corporate shell.

The strongest lesson for Ben is **current professional identity plus visible evidence of generosity and craft**. Use actual project decisions, selected books/talks, and an authentic personal introduction. Avoid inventing a newsletter schedule, current conference circuit, testimonials, or a new manifesto to fill visual space.

## Earlier concepts — superseded visual directions

The following three directions were useful editorial starting points, but they do not reflect the later reference set supplied for this assessment. In particular, **no serif direction should be taken forward**. The revised concepts below are the decision set for the next design iteration.

### 1. The Practitioner’s Portfolio — recommended

**Idea:** the considered, approachable voice of someone who has built, led, and taught. An employer-facing portfolio with the reading quality of a personal publication, independent of posting frequency.

**Visual language:** warm paper `#F5F1E8`, ink `#272824`, oxblood `#8C3D32`, quiet stone rules. Use one all-sans family such as Manrope, Geist, or Söhne (subject to licensing), with weight, scale, and spacing creating hierarchy. Large but calm type, asymmetric columns, generous margins, descriptive captions, minimal borders. A simple “Ben Dewey” typographic nameplate. No faux signature.

**Opening copy:** “Leading teams. Shaping products. Making technology useful.” Supporting copy introduces Ben in the first person as a product and technology leader connecting discovery, design, architecture, and delivery. Primary action: Explore my work. Secondary: About Ben; résumé remains in the header.

**Homepage order:** personal introduction → two selected work stories → selected writing/book and talk → how I work → founder connection → contact. Feature a real relaxed portrait or work-in-context photograph next to the introduction once selected. A small dated note can reveal current interests without requiring frequent publishing.

**Case studies:** long-form editorial reading, a concise role/scope panel, selected decisions, approved artifacts, and honest delivered/proposed outcomes. Pull out one explanatory diagram or lesson; avoid a wall of technology badges.

**Mobile and motion:** collapse the margin notes beneath their related paragraphs; keep work titles readable; simple link interactions, no scroll choreography. The reading experience carries the identity.

**Why it fits Ben:** joins leadership maturity with the author and educator history. It remains credible with a small, carefully maintained content collection. Nuology stays visibly separate through warm materials, an all-sans type system, and a quieter composition.

**Tradeoff:** could become a generic executive journal if it lacks personal photographs and specific technical decisions. Those assets are part of the concept, not optional decoration.

### 2. The Open Workshop

**Idea:** let visitors see how Ben turns an unclear problem into a product direction and a workable delivery path.

**Visual language:** porcelain `#F2F5F0`, deep forest `#183E34`, moss `#CAD8B9`, restrained amber accents. Humanist sans-serif such as IBM Plex Sans, with IBM Plex Mono only for figure labels and annotations. Structured work tables, schematic fragments, paper-like artifacts, and clear section numbering. Use real workshop/whiteboard imagery rather than stock circuit boards.

**Opening copy:** “Find the problem. Shape the product. Bring the team.” A short personal introduction sits alongside a compact three-stage process diagram: Understand → Shape → Deliver. This is a proposed explanation of working style, not a proprietary framework claim.

**Homepage order:** personal intro and approach → featured product-leadership case → compact work index → writing/teaching → currently building at Nuology → contact. Résumé and employer-relevant evidence remain easy to find.

**Case studies:** organize around Problem / My role / Decisions / Evidence / What happened. Surface the difference between shipped work, discovery, and recommendations. A later optional disclosure can expose a decision detail; the summary must work without interaction.

**Mobile and motion:** process steps become a vertical sequence; work rows wrap into readable blocks; optional diagrams never require horizontal scrolling. No draggable workspace or terminal interface.

**Why it fits Ben:** expresses the uncommon mix of design facilitation, product ownership, architecture, and delivery. Connects naturally to founder work through practice rather than matching Nuology’s styling.

**Tradeoff:** needs approved artifacts and well-written decision stories. Too many diagrams or process labels would make it feel like another consultancy. Keep Ben’s voice and picture prominent.

### 3. The Public Forum

**Idea:** a memorable personal identity for a technical leader whose work includes building understanding in other people.

**Visual language:** pale lavender `#F2EDF5`, aubergine `#37233F`, deep berry `#98444F`, soft peach `#E9C5AE`. Oversized name typography, a clear sans-serif such as Manrope, magazine-like typography, real stage/workshop photography, and a few generous color fields. A name-led masthead replaces any corporate symbol.

**Opening copy:** “Build useful things. Help people understand them.” A large Ben Dewey nameplate sits above the introduction. Primary action remains Selected work; Speaking is a prominent secondary route rather than the main conversion goal.

**Homepage order:** personal identity → featured leadership story → speaking/writing pair → community contribution and dated recognition → founder/product note → contact. A genuine stage photograph can anchor the lower half without dominating the employer introduction.

**Case studies:** concise visual essays with a strong summary and supporting evidence. Speaking pages include dated recordings/slides, short and long bios, approved photos, past community involvement, and an inquiry route. Proposed future talk topics must be labeled as proposed until Ben confirms them.

**Mobile and motion:** large name reflows without clipping, content pairs stack, and historical dates stay next to artifacts. No autoplay reel, looping marquee, or scrolling word carousel.

**Why it fits Ben:** makes the author, mentor, speaker, and community history visible immediately while supporting leadership work. The plum palette and editorial name treatment create the greatest distance from Nuology’s technical corporate presentation.

**Tradeoff:** most dependent on good personal/event photography and curated public evidence. If dominated by the speaker identity it could obscure the employer-facing goal.

## Revised direction from the supplied references

The Squarespace examples, [Steven Bartlett](https://stevenbartlett.com/), [Mel Robbins](https://www.melrobbins.com/), and [M&P Presentations](https://www.mppresentations.com/) point to a different and more suitable visual register: a confident person-led homepage, generous photography, very simple navigation, all-sans typography, and clear routes to a personal story and direct contact.

The useful pattern is not a speaker-sales site. Bartlett connects a personal story to current roles and ventures; Robbins organizes a broad body of work into a small number of recognizable paths; M&P makes the people and inquiry path obvious. Ben’s version should make current leadership work, selected evidence, and the person behind it easy to understand. It should not depend on testimonial carousels, a booking funnel, aggressive audience claims, or an artificial publishing cadence.

### What to borrow

- A strong real portrait or work-in-context image in the opening screen. Photography supplies the human presence the committed concept lacks.
- A compact top navigation: **Work, Meet Ben, Writing & Speaking, Contact**, plus a résumé utility. Nuology belongs within Meet Ben or selected work, not as a competing top-level brand.
- An immediate personal proposition, followed by proof and a short story. The visitor should understand Ben before seeing an archive or a long project grid.
- High-contrast, editorial color blocks and an all-sans system. Use type scale, weight, and white space for character; do not bring serif display faces back into the exploration.
- A short, human contact route. “Contact Ben” is more appropriate to this brief than “Book Ben.”

### 1. The Personal Portfolio — recommended

**Idea:** an assured introduction to the person, then a clear record of leadership, products, and public contribution.

**Visual language:** all-sans typography with a compact, distinctive wordmark; creamy white backgrounds, deep charcoal type, and a restrained tomato or brick-red accent. Use a wide portrait crop beside or behind the opening statement, large solid color fields, direct labels, and generous spacing. The palette and composition deliberately avoid Nuology’s black-and-electric-blue technical language.

**Homepage:** a concise first-person leadership statement and portrait → selected work → Meet Ben → writing, books, and speaking evidence → a short Nuology founder note → contact. The navigation keeps each route plain and readable.

**Voice:** “I lead teams building useful digital products.” Supporting copy can name the thread from technical work and community contributions to former leadership roles, without pretending the blog is current.

**Why it fits:** it gives the employer audience a fast, human answer to who Ben is while leaving space for author, speaker, and founder evidence. It uses the editorial confidence of the references without copying their layouts or conversion model.

### 2. The Story & Work

**Idea:** a personal career narrative whose chapters lead into selected work, rather than a conventional résumé page with a portfolio bolted on.

**Visual language:** all-sans headline and body typography; warm off-white, black, faded coral, and a pale sage supporting surface. The page moves through bold vertical chapters with real portrait, workshop, and archive imagery. Details such as dates, books, and talks act as captions rather than decorative badges.

**Homepage:** introduction → “Meet Ben” timeline (builder, teacher/community contributor, product leader, former EY team leader, founder) → three selected work moments → writing and speaking archive → contact. Each chapter has one sentence of context and one evidence link.

**Why it fits:** it takes the narrative clarity seen on Bartlett’s site but keeps the emphasis on professional substance. It is the best option if Ben wants the return from a management-heavy period to feel intentional rather than explained away.

**Risk:** the story needs a disciplined edit. A timeline is useful only if every entry earns its place through a relevant role, artifact, or link.

### 3. The Simple Introduction

**Idea:** a quieter, high-trust personal site built around a single clear introduction and two strong calls to action: Meet Ben and Contact Ben.

**Visual language:** all-sans typography only; white and soft stone surfaces, dense black text, and one unexpected but restrained accent such as chartreuse or vermilion. A single excellent portrait carries most of the visual identity. Sections use clean rules and simple two-column editorial layouts instead of cards and dashboards.

**Homepage:** name and one-sentence proposition → Meet Ben → selected work → a compact “Writing & Speaking” list → contact. The site can launch credibly with two work stories and a small set of dated archive links.

**Why it fits:** it adopts M&P’s directness and the clarity of the Squarespace references. It is the strongest option when the available public evidence is selective and the goal is an approachable professional introduction.

**Risk:** it depends on excellent writing and photography. Without them, simplicity can feel sparse rather than deliberate.

### Decision

Move forward with **The Personal Portfolio**, borrowing the narrative sequence of **The Story & Work** for the Meet Ben page. This gives the homepage its needed human presence, retains a practical employer-facing structure, and creates a natural location for the historical technical archive without overstating recent publishing.

## Common launch requirements

All three concepts use the same lightweight application plan. Design should work with two or three strong stories; a complete inventory is secondary. Keep visible navigation to five or six choices, provide a recruiter-friendly résumé, and make contact a short path. Put access to the fuller inventory on Work, not in the main hero. Explain additional context there in ordinary language.

Choose one portrait, one authentic working/speaking image, an approved representative artifact per featured case, and a small set of source-linked books/talks. Record exact roles, public labels, dates, and publication approvals before production copy is finalized. Current MVP status, present speaking availability, and final résumé totals remain open editorial questions.

Check keyboard use, visible focus, contrast, zoom, small-screen reading, image alternatives, reduced motion, and date clarity during implementation. These exploratory concepts are not accessibility-certified production interfaces.

Select a single visual system. The recommended combination is warm editorial Portfolio presentation with Workshop-style case-study questions; mixing all three palettes, type systems, and layouts would weaken the personal identity.
